# Case617: equalities are consistent, but nonnegative occupancy is impossible

**Claim ceiling.** A simple graph on17vertices with no clique of size5, no independent set of size4, and maximum degree at most7 cannot have the C188 neighborhood class617 at a selected degree6root. This is one restricted local exclusion, not a decision of the full17vertex target, a global Ramsey result, a census reproduction, or a novelty claim. Review is internal; no blind, external, or formal verification is asserted.

## 1. Graph-to-occupancy reduction

The named C186 cumulative refinement proves that every degree in a possible target is6or7. Its parity addendum ensures that at least one vertex has degree6. Choose such a root. Let A be its six neighbors and B the remaining ten vertices. The ledger's local labels0,...,5 name A only, corresponding to global labels1,...,6; the global root is outside A.

As checked in C187, B has no independent triple, since the root would extend one to an independent four-set. For any u in B, its nonneighbors within B form a clique of size at most4. Thus its degree within B is at least5, and the full degree bound7 leaves at most2 neighbors in A.

For a in A, write k[a] for its degree within A and t[a] for its number of neighbors in B. The full degree lower bound6 gives t[a]>=5-k[a]. When A has5edges, total cross incidence T satisfies T>=30-10=20. Ten B vertices with at most2 A neighbors give T<=20. Equality follows: every B vertex has exactly2 A neighbors, and t[a]=5-k[a] for each a.

Let x[S] count B vertices with exact A-neighbor pair S. These counts are nonnegative integers, hence in particular nonnegative real numbers. There are15 unordered pairs. They satisfy total count10 and the six incidence equations with right sides5-k[a]. If an independent triple Q in A is disjoint from S, then a vertex of type S together with Q would be an independent four-set. Consequently x[S]=0 for each such forbidden pair. These constraints are necessary, not sufficient for a B-graph completion.

## 3. Nonnegativity contradiction

The isolate has required crossdegree5. Exactly five of the ten B vertices are therefore adjacent to it, and the other five must all have pair type04. Hence x[04]=5. The degree equation for local vertex0 requires x[04]+x[05]=3 after its other incident pair types are forbidden. Consequently x[05]=-2, contradicting nonnegativity. This does not require pair multiplicity caps or an integer-programming search.

There is also a redundant stronger obstruction available from C187's edge-pair cap2, since04 is an edge. It is not needed or used as a deciding dependency here. The present proof relies only on the support and incidence equations with nonnegative variables.

## 4. Exact cone certificate and sign convention

Let Mx=b denote the16 equations: one total-count row, six degree rows, and nine forbidden-type zero rows, with15 lexicographically ordered pair columns. Take the degree0 row minus the total row plus the degree5 row plus each forbidden-type row whose pair omits vertex0. There are six such forbidden-type rows. The resulting coefficient vector is exactly the unit vector for column05, and the right side is3-10+5=-2.

For any real M,b and row vector y, if y^T M is componentwise nonnegative and y^T b is strictly negative, then Mx=b has no solution with x>=0. Indeed 0<=(y^T M)x=y^T b<0 would follow. Our certificate has y^T M=e[05] and y^T b=-2. The implication is elementary; no converse theorem, numerical solver status, floating precision, or tolerance is imported.

Equality-row multipliers y may have either sign. The consumer uses the stated canonical orientation: combined column coefficients nonnegative, right side negative. Positive integer rescaling preserves that condition. Negating y fails this particular orientation; that does not invalidate the mathematical exclusion, since negating again restores the accepted orientation. A negative combined column with a negative right side is insufficient: for example -x=-2 has the admissible nonnegative solution x=2. This is a deciding sign canary.

## 5. Why the previous equality-only method cannot settle this case

The equality system has a signed integer solution: x[04]=5, x[05]=-2, x[15]=2, x[25]=3, x[35]=3, x[45]=-1, and all nine forbidden counts0. The total is10; all six incidence sums are3,2,3,3,4,5. Thus it is a real solution of Mx=b, while violating the required count domain.

An equality-only row refutation y^T M=0 with y^T b!=0 is impossible for this M,b: multiplying by the displayed real solution would force y^T b=0. This is a proved limitation of that certificate family, not merely an unsuccessful search. The signed vector is not a valid occupancy histogram and not a graph counterexample. Retaining it as a healthy equality witness and rejecting it as nonnegative occupancy tests the domain distinction directly.
