# Case 632: a support cover exceeds the available incidence

**Claim ceiling.** A simple graph on 17 vertices with no clique of size 5, no independent set of size 4, and maximum degree at most 7 cannot have C188 neighborhood class 632 at a selected degree-six root. This is one local class exclusion. It does not decide the full target, reproduce a source census, establish novelty, or supply external or formal review.

## Graph-to-pair reduction

The actual C186 cumulative minimum-degree refinement and parity corollary show that a possible target has all degrees six or seven and admits a degree-six root. Let A be its six neighbors and B the ten remaining vertices. Local ledger labels 0 through 5 name A only, corresponding to global labels 1 through 6; the global root is outside A.

B has no independent triple, because the root would extend one to an independent four-set. For any vertex of B, its nonneighbors in B form a clique of size at most four. Its degree within B is therefore at least five, leaving at most two neighbors in A under the maximum-degree bound seven.

Write k[a] for induced degree in A and t[a] for crossdegree into B. The full minimum degree six gives t[a] >= 5-k[a]. If A has five edges, total cross incidence is at least 30-2*5 = 20. Ten B vertices with at most two A neighbors give at most 20. Thus every B vertex has exactly two A neighbors and every individual lower bound is attained: t[a] = 5-k[a]. This is the named C189 saturation argument, rederived here; no numerical search or imported equality-case assumption is needed.

Let x[S] count vertices of B with exact A-neighbor pair S. Counts are nonnegative integers, hence nonnegative reals. They sum to 10 and have incidence degrees t[a]. An occupied pair must meet every independent triple Q in A; otherwise its B vertex and Q form an independent four-set. Any pair avoiding such a triple is forbidden and has count zero. These constraints are necessary, not sufficient for a B-graph completion.

## A weighted support-cover lemma

More generally, let a finite family of nonempty types S carry nonnegative real counts x[S] with total N. Define incidence degrees t[a] = sum_{S containing a} x[S]. Suppose nonnegative real vertex weights w[a] satisfy sum_{a in S} w[a] >= 1 for every allowed type. Then

sum_a w[a]t[a]
= sum_S x[S] sum_{a in S} w[a]
>= sum_S x[S] = N.

All sums are finite; no convergence, integrality, or optimization converse is involved. A weight vector satisfying all support constraints but having weighted degree total less than N therefore proves infeasibility. Testing only a subset of allowed types is insufficient: an omitted uncovered type may carry the entire count. The lemma applies to all weights satisfying its stated hypotheses; the finite instrument only checks the supplied integer weights for this case.

## Complete support for the actual graph

Class 632 has edges 04,05,12,13,23: a path 4-0-5 and a disjoint triangle on 1,2,3. Induced degrees are (2,2,2,2,1,1), so crossdegrees are (3,3,3,3,4,4). There is no isolated vertex, and the earlier isolate proof is not being transplanted.

A triangle contributes at most one vertex to an independent set. In the path, the only independent pair is {4,5}. Consequently the complete independent-triple list is {1,4,5}, {2,4,5}, {3,4,5}.

A pair meeting neither 4 nor 5 lies in {0,1,2,3}. To meet all three triples it would have to contain all of 1,2,3, impossible for a pair. Conversely every pair containing 4 or 5 meets all three triples. Thus exactly nine pairs are allowed: 04,05,14,15,24,25,34,35,45. The other six pairs are forbidden. The consumer verifies all 15 columns, all three triples, the complete forbidden list, and an actual disjoint triple for each exclusion.

Assign weights w[4]=w[5]=1 and all other weights zero. Every allowed pair has weight at least one; the pair 45 has weight two. The weighted degree total is t[4]+t[5]=4+4=8. The support-cover lemma requires at least 10. This contradiction excludes the selected neighborhood class.

## Exact row certificate

Write Mx=b for the total-count row, six degree rows, and six forbidden-type zero rows, in 15 lexicographic pair columns. There are 13 rows. Take the degree-4 row plus the degree-5 row minus the total row plus all six forbidden rows.

For a pair containing exactly one of 4,5, the coefficient is 1-1=0. For pair 45 it is 2-1=1. For each forbidden pair it is -1+1=0. The resulting column vector is the unit vector for pair 45, while the right side is 4+4-10=-2. Thus x[45]=-2.

Equivalently, y^T M is componentwise nonnegative and y^T b=-2<0. For x>=0, the identity (y^T M)x=y^T b is impossible. Row multipliers may have either sign; the canonical combined-column/right-side condition is what matters. A negative column and negative right side alone would not suffice: -x=-2 has the nonnegative solution x=2. The generic cone arithmetic is reused from the reviewed, hash-pinned C190 implementation, not described as a new independent arithmetic checker.

## Equality-only limitation and admissibility

The equality system has a signed integer solution: x[04]=3, x[14]=3, x[25]=3, x[35]=3, x[45]=-2, all other counts zero. Its total is 10; vertex degrees are 3,3,3,3,4,4, and every forbidden count is zero. It is a valid real solution of Mx=b, but not a nonnegative occupancy histogram or a graph.

As in C190, existence of this real solution proves that an equality-only contradiction y^T M=0 with y^T b!=0 is impossible for this specific model. The failed route is proved inapplicable rather than merely unsuccessfully searched. Nonnegativity resolves the selected obstruction. Other mechanisms and the parent graph problem are unaffected by that route limitation.
