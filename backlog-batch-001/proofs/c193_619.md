# A mixed-type clique bound excludes neighborhood class 619

**PROVED, restricted local statement.** There is no simple graph on 17 vertices with no K5, no independent four-set, maximum degree at most seven, and a selected degree-six root whose six-vertex neighborhood has edges 01, 02, 04, 12, 13, 23. Labels 0–5 in this proof belong to that neighborhood, not to the root.

This is a local project lemma, with internal analytic and exact computational checks. It does not decide the entire 17-vertex target or a global Ramsey number. Novelty, blind reproduction, external review, and formal verification are not established.

## Dependencies and admissible types

The pinned C186 refinement proves every vertex has degree six or seven under the stated graph assumptions. Its separate parity corollary selects a degree-six root. Put A equal to that root's six neighbors and B equal to its ten nonneighbors. The pinned C187 proof gives the following elementary necessary conditions; the important arguments are repeated here.

A has no K4, since the root would extend it to K5. B has no independent triple, since the root would extend it to an independent four-set. For u in B, its nonneighbors inside B form a clique of size at most four. Hence degree_B(u) is at least five, and u has at most two neighbors in A.

If two B vertices both avoid a nonedge a,b of A, they must be adjacent, or those four vertices would be independent. An empty A-neighborhood type is impossible: for any other B vertex w, A minus N_A(w) has at least four vertices and therefore contains a nonedge. An empty-type vertex u and w both avoid that nonedge and must be adjacent. Thus u would have all nine other B vertices as neighbors, contrary to the maximum degree seven.

Consequently every B vertex has a singleton or pair type S=N_A(u). Let n[S] count its multiplicity. For a vertex a in A with induced degree k[a], its full degree is 1+k[a]+t[a], where t[a]=sum_{a in S} n[S]. Thus

    5-k[a] <= t[a] <= 6-k[a].

Every type meets every independent triple in A. The counts are nonnegative integers summing to ten. The instrument relaxes integrality to nonnegative reals, so an obstruction to that relaxation also excludes actual graphs.

## The common-neighbor bound

For any nonedge a,b in A and any other vertex z in A, let C be the B vertices avoiding a,b and adjacent to z. The preceding independent-set argument makes C a clique. Since z is adjacent to all of C, four members of C together with z would give K5. Therefore

    sum_{S: z in S, a not in S, b not in S} n[S] <= 3.

This holds for all the stated neighborhoods, independently of their edge count. It combines different types; separate per-type caps do not imply it.

## Class 619

Here vertex 5 is isolated in A. Among vertices 0–4 the nonedges are exactly 03, 14, 24, 34. There is no independent triple on those five vertices: 0 has only 3 as a nonneighbor, 1 and 2 each only 4, and the nonneighbors 0,4 of 3 are adjacent. Thus the independent triples of A are exactly 035, 145, 245, 345.

The only singleton meeting all four triples is {5}. Every pair containing 5 meets them. A pair without 5 must meet each of 03,14,24,34. If it contains 4, its other member is 0 or 3; if it omits 4, the conditions 14 and 24 force the pair 12, which misses 03. The complete allowed support is therefore

    {5}, {0,4}, {0,5}, {1,5}, {2,5}, {3,4}, {3,5}, {4,5}.

Vertices 1 and 2 each have induced degree three. The only allowed type containing 1 is 15, and the only one containing 2 is 25. Their lower degree bounds force n[15]>=2 and n[25]>=2.

Apply the common-neighbor bound with (a,b,z)=(0,3,5). It says

    n[5] + n[15] + n[25] + n[45] <= 3.

All counts are nonnegative, but the middle two already sum to at least four. This is the contradiction 4<=3. No numerical tolerance, positivity limit, logarithm, solver status, or integrality theorem is involved.

## Why the new cut discriminates

The base necessary model also includes each singleton/nonedge-pair cap three, each edge-pair cap two, and all nine bounds stating that at most four B vertices avoid a particular A nonedge. The histogram

    n[04]=n[15]=n[25]=n[34]=n[35]=2; all other types zero

satisfies that whole base model. Its crossdegrees are (2,2,2,4,4,6), within the required lower and upper bounds. The exact consumer verifies every base inequality. This histogram is not claimed to have a graph realization. It violates the new common-neighbor bound because n[15]+n[25]=4>3. Thus feasibility of the older relaxation cannot settle this class, and the new combined bound supplies information that it lacked.

## Exact certificate and membership

The retained model contains all 21 singleton/pair variables, 14 equality rows, and 43 inequality rows. Adding a nonnegative slack for each inequality gives 57 equations in 64 nonnegative variables. Sum the lower-degree rows for 1 and 2 and the common-neighbor row. Use the recorded forbidden-type zero rows to cancel any negative forbidden-variable coefficients. The resulting left coefficients are all nonnegative, while the right side is -1. The saved multiplier vector and matrix certify precisely that nonnegative equality infeasibility.
