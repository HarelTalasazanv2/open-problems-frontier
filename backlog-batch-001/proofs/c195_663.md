# Class 663 is excluded by the base model

**PROVED, restricted local statement.** No simple graph on 17 vertices with no K5, no independent four-set, maximum degree at most seven, and a selected degree-six root has the six-vertex neighborhood with edges 01, 02, 03, 05, 14, 23. Labels 0–5 denote that neighborhood A; the root r is separate. No full 17-vertex or global Ramsey decision is claimed.

The references and attribution (historical project reference) identify the existing graph lemmas, source readings, and accurately scoped research credit. The [base model](../data/c195_base_model.json), [certificate](../data/c195_base_certificate.json), result (historical project reference), and [residual register](../data/c195_residual.json) are saved inside the project.

## Necessary types

Let B be the ten nonneighbors of r. A contains no K4, since r would extend it to K5. B contains no independent triple, since r would extend it to an independent four-set. For u in B, its nonneighbors within B form a clique of size at most four. Thus u has at least five B-neighbors and, by maximum total degree seven, at most two A-neighbors.

Write S for a B vertex's A-neighborhood. Every S meets every independent triple of A. In this class those triples are exactly 125, 135, 245, 345. To check completeness: a triple containing 0 is impossible because 0 has only one A-nonneighbor, namely 4. On 1–5 the only edges are 14 and 23; an independent triple must use 5 and one endpoint from each of those two edges.

An empty S misses all four triples and cannot occur. The singleton/pair types meeting all four are exactly

\[
5,\quad05,\quad14,\quad15,\quad23,\quad25,\quad35,\quad45.
\]

Indeed every singleton/pair containing 5 works. A pair omitting 5 must meet all four cross-pairs 12, 13, 24, 34; the only possibilities are 14 and 23. This exhausts the allowed types, without assuming that all B vertices have two A-neighbors.

## The base contradiction

B vertices of any fixed type S form a clique. To see this, A minus S has at least four vertices, so it contains a nonedge a,b because A has no K4. Two nonadjacent B vertices of type S would form an independent four-set with a,b. If S is an edge of A, three B vertices of that type, together with the two vertices of S, would give K5. Therefore

\[
n[14]\le2,\qquad n[23]\le2.
\]

These are the only allowed types omitting 5. Population ten consequently implies

\[
t[5]=10-n[14]-n[23]\ge6.
\]

But A-vertex 5 is a leaf, adjacent only to 0. Its full degree is \(1+1+t[5]\): one root edge, one edge in A, and its B-neighbors. The maximum degree seven gives \(t[5]\le5\). This is the contradiction \(6\le5\).

No new common-neighbor cut, graph completion search, solver status, integrality theorem, or minimum-degree lower bound is used in that argument. The degree-six root is a stated hypothesis. The existing C186 parity/selection corollary is a separate dependency when using this local exclusion in the wider root-classification program.

## Exact saved base certificate

For consistency with the native model, retain all 21 singleton/pair variables, population ten, all 13 forbidden-type zero equalities, six lower and six upper crossdegree bounds, all 21 type capacities, and all nine A-nonedge avoidance bounds. The induced degree vector is (4,2,2,2,1,1); the base intervals are \(5-k[a]\le t[a]\le6-k[a]\). The lower endpoints are inherited from the separate minimum-degree lemma, but their certificate multipliers are all zero.

Lift the 42 inequalities with their own nonnegative slacks, obtaining 56 equations in 63 nonnegative real variables. Sum the rows for upper:5, cap:14, cap:23 and subtract the population row. Add each of the 13 forbidden-type zero rows with coefficient one. In every original type column the coefficient is zero. In the three selected slack columns the coefficient is one; all other slack coefficients are zero. The right side is

\[
5+2+2-10=-1.
\]

Thus the complete saved system satisfies \(M^Ty\ge0\) and \(y^Tb=-1\), which contradicts \(Mx=b\), \(x\ge0\). Equality-row multipliers may be negative; the lifted slack columns enforce nonnegative multipliers on the inequality rows. Applying this argument to signed variables would be invalid.

The alternate checker reconstructs the graph and incidence rows from literal bitsets and uses exact Fraction arithmetic. It also deletes all six zero-weight lower-degree rows and their six slack columns. The resulting 50-by-57 system has the same negative right side. This confirms that the minimum-degree lower bound is unnecessary to this exclusion, although it remains part of the historical base schema.

## Quantitative residual margin

For this fixed, unscaled 56-by-63 representation the saved multiplier has \(\|y\|_1=17\). For every nonnegative real 63-vector x,

\[
y^T(Mx-b)=x^TM^Ty-y^Tb\ge1.
\]

The elementary triangle inequality gives
\(|y^T(Mx-b)|\le17\|Mx-b\|_\infty\). Hence

\[
\|Mx-b\|_\infty\ge1/17.
\]

This is a lower bound on the residual, not its exact minimum, a distance to a graph, or an invariant under arbitrary row scaling. Positive rescaling of the entire multiplier leaves the ratio unchanged. The newly inspected norm-approximation and equality-reformulation slides motivated this check; the nonnegative-domain implication is derived here directly rather than imported from an unconstrained dual formula.

## Orbit, challenges, and limits

The unique degree-four vertex 0 is fixed by every automorphism. Leaf 5 is distinguished from leaf 4 by the degree of its neighbor; vertex 1 is then distinguished as 4's neighbor. Only triangle vertices 2 and 3 may be interchanged. The stabilizer therefore has size two, giving 6!/2=360 labeled versions. Both forward-edge permutations and a separate adjacency-permutation implementation evaluate all 720 permutations and match the exact C188 class membership.

Only that complete orbit is removed: 52 classes /15,682 labeled neighborhoods become **51 /15,322**. No retained class is thereby declared feasible. The full target, global Ramsey value, novelty, external review and formal verification remain unestablished by this run.
