# Class 633 is excluded: two exact local proofs

**PROVED at restricted local scope.** No simple graph on 17 vertices with no K5, no independent four-set, maximum degree at most seven, and a selected degree-six root has neighborhood edges 01, 04, 05, 12, 13, 23. Labels 0–5 in this proof belong to the root's neighborhood A; the root itself is r. This does not solve the entire 17-vertex target or a global Ramsey problem. Novelty, external review, blind reproduction and formal verification are not established.

## Precise setup and complete support

Put A=N(r), so |A|=6, and let B be the ten nonneighbors of r. B has no independent triple: such a triple together with r would be an independent four-set. For any u in B, its nonneighbors inside B form a clique of size at most four; otherwise a nonadjacent pair among them with u would be an independent triple. Hence degree_B(u)>=5. The maximum full degree seven implies |N_A(u)|<=2.

For this A the independent triples are exactly 145, 245, 345. To check completeness, a triple containing 0 cannot be independent, since the only A-nonneighbors of 0 are 2 and 3 and they are adjacent. Among the other five vertices, 1,2,3 form a triangle, while 4 and 5 are mutually nonadjacent and adjacent to none of 1,2,3. Thus an independent triple must contain 4,5 and exactly one of 1,2,3.

Every B vertex's A-neighborhood S must meet all three triples. If S omitted both 4 and 5 it would have to contain all of 1,2,3, contrary to |S|<=2. An empty type is also excluded by these triples. The complete allowed support is therefore

    {4}, {5}, {0,4}, {0,5}, {1,4}, {1,5},
    {2,4}, {2,5}, {3,4}, {3,5}, {4,5}.

Let n[S]>=0 be the type multiplicities and t[a]=sum_{a in S} n[S]. Actual graph multiplicities are integers; both obstructions also hold for the corresponding nonnegative real relaxation. Population is ten. If k[a] is the degree inside A, full degree of a is 1+k[a]+t[a]. Here k=(3,3,2,2,1,1). The full model uses degree intervals 5-k[a]<=t[a]<=6-k[a]; the lower endpoints use C186, while upper endpoints follow directly from maximum degree seven.

If a,b are nonadjacent in A, B vertices avoiding both form a clique. Otherwise two nonadjacent such B vertices with a,b would form an independent four-set. At most four B vertices can therefore avoid a,b.

## Proof 1: the base constraints already suffice

The allowed types containing 1 are exactly 14 and 15. Those containing 3 are exactly 34 and 35. These groups of B vertices are disjoint and every member avoids both 0 and 2. Since k[1]=3 and k[3]=2, the minimum full degree six implies t[1]>=2 and t[3]>=3.

Thus at least five B vertices avoid both 0 and 2. But 02 is an A-nonedge, so the preceding clique bound permits at most four. This is the contradiction 5<=4.

Equivalently, add the inequalities -t[1]<=-2, -t[3]<=-3 and avoidance(0,2)<=4. On the complete allowed support their left side is n[4]+n[5]+n[45]. This yields

    n[4] + n[5] + n[45] <= -1,

contrary to nonnegativity. The forbidden-type zero rows account for all other model columns. No common-neighbor strengthening is needed for this proof.

## Proof 2: three common-neighbor cuts, without minimum degree

Let C be the B vertices not adjacent to 4. Since vertex 4 has its root neighbor and its one A-neighbor 0, its maximum full degree implies t[4]<=5. Therefore |C|>=10-5=5.

Every member of C has A-neighbor 5 and at most one other A-neighbor, by the complete support and |S|<=2. In particular it is adjacent to at most one of 1,2,3 and avoids at least two of them.

For j in {1,2,3}, let C_j be the members of C avoiding j. The pair j,4 is an A-nonedge. Hence C_j is a clique, by the independent-four-set argument. Vertex 5 is adjacent to every member of C_j, so |C_j|<=3: four members with vertex 5 would give K5.

Counting membership in these three sets in two ways gives

    10 <= 2|C| <= |C_1|+|C_2|+|C_3| <= 9,

a contradiction. The same argument is a finite weighted-sum inequality for nonnegative real type masses. It uses no minimum-degree lower bound and makes no assumption that type 45 has zero mass or that all types are pairs.

Algebraically, sum the three common cuts (1,4,5), (2,4,5), (3,4,5), add twice t[4]<=5, and subtract twice the equality sum n[S]=10. On the complete allowed support the result is

    n[5] + n[05] <= 9+10-20 = -1.

This is a second nonnegativity contradiction. In the full certificate all lower-degree row multipliers are zero. A separately saved model removes all six lower-degree inequalities, and its saved certificate still verifies.

## Whole orbit and precise residual

Vertices 0 and 1 are the two degree-three vertices. Vertex 0 has two degree-one neighbors; vertex 1 has none, so every automorphism fixes both. The two degree-two vertices 2,3 can be interchanged, as can the two degree-one vertices 4,5. All four such permutations are automorphisms and there are no others. Orbit-stabilizer therefore gives 6!/4=180 labeled versions.

The finite checker independently evaluates all 720 label permutations and compares the exact sorted member set against the pinned C188 class, not merely its size. Only this verified orbit is removed from the C193 residual. The result is 54 classes / 15,952 labeled neighborhoods, down from 55 / 16,132. Remaining classes are not asserted to be feasible, and the full graph target remains unresolved.
