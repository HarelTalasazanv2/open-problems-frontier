# Class 635: base feasibility, then a clique obstruction

**PROVED at restricted local scope:** no simple graph on 17 vertices with no K5, no independent four-set, maximum degree seven, and a selected degree-six root can have neighborhood edges 01, 02, 04, 05, 12, 13, 23. Labels 0–5 name that neighborhood A, not the root r. This does not decide the full 17-vertex target or a global Ramsey number.

## Setup

Let B be the ten nonneighbors of r. B has no independent triple. For u in B, its B-nonneighbors form a clique of size at most four; hence degree_B(u)>=5 and its A-neighborhood S has size at most two.

The independent triples of A are exactly 145, 245, 345. Vertex 0 has only 3 as an A-nonneighbor, so cannot belong to an independent triple. Vertices 1,2,3 form a triangle, and 4,5 are mutually nonadjacent and avoid all of 1,2,3. This proves the list is complete.

Every type S must meet all three triples. With |S|<=2, that forces S to meet {4,5}; otherwise it would have to contain all three of 1,2,3. Thus the eleven allowed types are singletons 4,5 and all nine pairs meeting {4,5}. Empty type is impossible here because it misses an independent triple. All 21 singleton/pair variables are retained in the model, with the other ten constrained to zero.

## A separately frozen strengthening excludes the class

After that base result was saved and checked, the common-neighbor family was selected and separately frozen. For any A-nonedge a,b and third A-vertex z, B vertices avoiding a,b and adjacent to z form a clique. Four of them together with z would give K5, so their mass is at most three.

Let C be the B vertices avoiding 4. Vertex 4 has its root edge and its A-neighbor 0; maximum full degree seven gives t[4]<=5, hence |C|>=5. Each member of C has neighbor 5 and at most one additional A-neighbor, so it avoids at least two of 1,2,3.

For j=1,2,3, let C_j be the members of C avoiding j. The pair j,4 is an A-nonedge, so C_j is a clique. All its members are adjacent to 5, giving |C_j|<=3. Counting memberships yields

    10 <= 2|C| <= |C_1|+|C_2|+|C_3| <= 9,

a contradiction. This exclusion uses no minimum-degree lower bound once the degree-six root is fixed. It keeps singleton types and permits type 45; no pair-only saturation or graph realization of the base point is assumed.

Equivalently, sum the cuts (1,4,5), (2,4,5), (3,4,5), add twice t[4]<=5, and subtract twice the population equality. On allowed support this gives

    n[5] + n[05] <= 9+10-20 = -1.

Nonnegativity is impossible. The argument also applies to nonnegative real masses. The base witness violates four of the 32 strengthened cuts: (1,4,5), (1,5,4), (2,4,5), (2,5,4) each have mass four rather than at most three. The stronger model therefore adds information genuinely absent from the feasible base relaxation.

## Exact certificate and orbit

The complete strengthened model retains all 32 common cuts and lifts to 84 equations / 94 nonnegative variables. Removing all six lower-degree inequalities gives 78 equations / 88 variables. The sparse row multipliers have nonnegative combined columns and right side -1 in both models. If Mx=b with x>=0, those multipliers would imply 0<=y^T Mx=y^T b=-1. No numerical tolerance, solver status, integrality theorem or strong-duality assertion is needed.

All 720 relabelings produce exactly the 180 members of the pinned C188 orbit. Independently, vertex 0 is the unique degree-four vertex and 3 the unique degree-two vertex; both are fixed by automorphisms. Vertices 1,2 can be interchanged, as can 4,5, giving exactly four automorphisms and orbit size 6!/4=180. Only that complete orbit is removed: 54 classes / 15,952 labeled neighborhoods become 53 / 15,772. The remaining cases are not declared feasible.

Verification is same-actor and nonblind. Primary checks reuse previously verified exact arithmetic helpers; a separate literal-adjacency/bitset implementation reconstructs every row and uses rational arithmetic, and an alternate adjacency-permutation algorithm checks the orbit. This is not external or formal verification; novelty is unestablished.
