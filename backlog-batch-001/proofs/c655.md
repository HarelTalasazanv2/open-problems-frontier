# Class 655: base feasibility and a degree-dependent clique obstruction

**PROVED at restricted local scope:** no simple graph on 17 vertices with no K5, no independent four-set, maximum degree at most seven, and a selected degree-six root has neighborhood edges 01, 02, 03, 04, 14, 23. Labels 0–5 belong to the six-vertex neighborhood A; the root is r. The full 17-vertex target and global Ramsey questions are not decided.

## Support and base model

Let B be the ten nonneighbors of r. B has no independent triple. For u in B, its B-nonneighbors form a clique of size at most four; hence degree_B(u)>=5 and its A-neighborhood S has size at most two.

The independent triples of A are exactly 125,135,245,345. Vertex 0 meets every other vertex except 5. On vertices 1–4 there are just the edges 14 and 23, so an independent triple must consist of 5 and a cross-nonedge 12,13,24,34.

Every B type must meet all four triples. If it omits 5, it must hit all four cross-nonedges with at most two vertices. A type containing 1 must then also contain 4; a type omitting 1 must contain both 2 and 3. Thus the allowed types are exactly 5,05,14,15,23,25,35,45. Empty type is excluded by these triples. All 21 singleton/pair variables are kept; the other thirteen are constrained to zero.

For multiplicities n[S]>=0, population is ten and t[a]=sum_{a in S}n[S]. The induced degrees are k=(4,2,2,2,2,0), so the base intervals are 5-k[a]<=t[a]<=6-k[a]. The lower endpoints use the minimum-degree-six lemma; upper endpoints follow from maximum degree seven.

## Separately frozen clique strengthening

If a,b are an A-nonedge, B vertices avoiding both form a clique: a nonadjacent pair among them with a,b would be an independent four-set. If those B vertices also meet z in A, their mass is at most three, since four with z would be K5. The strengthened model includes every one of the 36 such common-neighbor bounds.

Only non-5 types 14 and 23 are allowed. Each has mass at most two: its B vertices form a clique by avoiding nonedge 05, and three of them with the corresponding A-edge would form K5. Population ten therefore gives t[5]>=6.

Sum the four common bounds (1,2,5), (1,3,5), (2,4,5), (3,4,5). A singleton5 or type05 is counted four times; every other 5-type is counted twice. Consequently

    2*t[5] + 2*(n[5]+n[05]) <= 12.

The minimum full degree six at A-vertex 0, whose induced degree is four, gives t[0]=n[05]>=1. The left side is therefore at least 2*6+2*1=14, contradicting the upper bound twelve.

In exact row form, sum those four common bounds, twice the caps on 14 and 23, minus twice the population equality, and twice the inequality -t[0]<=-1. On allowed support the result is

    2*n[5] <= 12+8-20-2 = -2.

This contradicts nonnegativity, including the boundary n[5]=0. Forbidden-type zero rows cancel any negative coefficients in the remaining original columns. Slack lifting gives 92 equations in 99 nonnegative variables. The saved multiplier has all combined columns nonnegative and right side -2; no solver tolerance or strong-duality claim is involved. The same finite-sum argument applies to real nonnegative masses.

The unchanged base witness violates precisely the four common bounds above, each with mass four instead of at most three. The strengthening adds information missing from the feasible base model.

## Why the minimum-degree dependency matters

This proof imports the local C186 theorem: under the original 17-vertex noK5/noI4/maximum-degree-seven assumptions, every degree is at least six. Its proof was re-read and analytically rechecked. Briefly, the nonneighbor-clique bound gives degree at least four. At degree four, all edges from A to B are forbidden by the maximum degree, producing an independent four-set. At degree five, each B vertex has at most one A-neighbor. Two disjoint A-nonedges would force fourteen distinct B vertices although there are eleven; the complement edge-intersection lemma then forces an independent triple in A. B splits into three clique groups of size at most three, again contradicting eleven vertices. The linked source contains the full five-vertex lemma and quantifier details.

The second histogram in the table satisfies every strengthened constraint except lower:0. It has crossdegrees (0,3,4,4,3,6) and satisfies the model obtained by deleting only -t[0]<=-1, retaining every other lower bound and all 36 clique cuts. That model lifts to 91 equations / 98 variables and its saved witness verifies exactly.

Thus deleting this single row restores relaxation feasibility. The point is not a graph and does not refute the minimum-degree theorem. It shows that the bound is essential to this relaxation's exclusion, unlike the earlier degree-independent class635 argument. Removing its multiplier from the certificate leaves right side zero, which the strict infeasibility check rejects.

## Orbit and verification limits

Vertex 0 is the unique degree-four vertex, and 5 the unique isolate. Automorphisms fix both and may interchange the two edges 14,23 and swap endpoints within each edge: exactly 2*2*2=8 automorphisms, giving orbit size 6!/8=90. Both forward-edge and alternate adjacency-permutation checks evaluate all 720 relabelings and match the exact C188 member set.

Only this complete orbit is removed: 53 classes / 15,772 labeled neighborhoods become 52 / 15,682. The remaining cases are not declared feasible. Verification is same-actor and nonblind, with shared inputs and named dependencies. A separate bitset reconstruction and exact rational arithmetic check the models, both feasible witnesses and the exclusion certificate. No novelty, external review or formal verification is claimed.
