## Frozen claim and exact primary mapping

The cycle-024 attempt packet (historical local record; not distributed here) fixes the claim, controls and stopping rule. On `I=(0,1)`, define `R(v)=∫_{I²}|v(s)-v(t)|²/|s-t| dsdt` and let `H^log(I)` use the Fourier transform of the **zero extension**. Chen and Weth, *The Dirichlet Problem for the Logarithmic Laplacian*, arXiv:1710.03416v6 (2019), PDF pp. 18–19, define `W(Ω)` by the regional form `b(u,Ω)=1/2∫_{Ω²}|u(x)-u(y)|² k(x-y)dxdy`, with `k(z)=c_N 1_{|z|<1}|z|^{-N}` at PDF p. 7, (2.9). Their Proposition 4.2 proves `C_c^∞(Ω)` dense in `W(Ω)` for bounded Lipschitz domains. Remark 4.3, using Proposition 6.1/Corollary 6.2's logarithmic boundary Hardy inequality, explicitly concludes that zero extension is an isomorphism `W(Ω)→H(Ω)` with equivalent norms. Equation (4.3) gives full-space energy = regional energy + boundary killing term. In one dimension, every bounded interval is Lipschitz and `c_1>0` only rescales the energy. The pairs in `I²` with `|s-t|≥1` are absent from their truncated kernel, but for an interval of arbitrary length `d` their untruncated contribution is at most `4d||v||²`, so this does not change the finite-value set. Their paper is written for real functions; for complex functions the real and imaginary parts decouple in `|v(x)-v(y)|²`, so the same domain conclusion follows componentwise.

Chen–Weth's `H(Ω)` uses zero extension and the full-space truncated `|x-y|^{-N}` difference energy. [Laptev–Weth, *Spectral properties of the logarithmic Laplacian*](https://www.ma.imperial.ac.uk/~alaptev/Papers/laptev-weth-2020-revised.pdf), Lemma 2.2 and Corollary 2.4, separately identify the full-space energy with the logarithmic Fourier form and establish the corresponding bounded-Lipschitz domain description. In dimension one, the Plancherel calculation below gives the same conclusion directly. These papers are primary prior art for the **functional-analytic equivalence**; they do not identify Bombieri's historical extended Weil form or address Suzuki's 2026 wording.

## Adversarial interval check

For `0<s<1/16`, the interval `sqrt(s)<t<1/2` is nonempty, and `1/t≤1/(t-s)`. Integrating `|v(s)|²≤2|v(s)-v(t)|²+2|v(t)|²` against `dt/t` gives `L(s)|v(s)|²≤2A(s)+2C(s)`, where `L(s)=1/2 log(1/s)-log2≥1/4 log(1/s)`. Tonelli bounds `∫_0^{1/16}A(s)ds≤R(v)`. Reversing integration in the other term gives `∫_0^{1/16}C(s)ds=∫_0^{1/2}|v(t)|² min(t²,1/16)dt/t≤(1/4)||v||²`. Thus the left-end weighted log integral is at most `8R+2||v||²`; reflection yields the analogous right-end estimate, and the remaining middle contributions are bounded by `L²`. In particular the boundary term `B=∫_0^1|v(s)|²[log(1/s)+log(1/(1-s))]ds` is finite whenever `R+||v||²` is finite. This proof is insensitive to complex phase and uses only nonnegative Tonelli integrals. It is an independently restated quantitative version of cycle 023, **not** an independent human review.

For the zero extension, ordered pairs in `T(v)=∫_{|h|<1}||v(·+h)-v(·)||²_{L²(R)}dh/|h|` split exactly into interior-interior pairs `R` and two oriented boundary crossings `2B`; hence `T=R+2B`. Plancherel with `vhat(z)=∫e^{-izx}v(x)dx` gives `T=(2π)^{-1}∫4J(|z|)|vhat(z)|²dz`, `J(r)=∫_0^r(1-cos u)du/u`. `J(r)=log r+O(1)` for `r≥1`, and is nonnegative/bounded near zero, so `1+J` is comparable to `1+log⁺r`. Therefore `R+||v||²`, `T+||v||²`, and the `H^log` norm have the same finite-value set on an interval. There is no floating-point tolerance or extrapolation from a grid.

Controls: `v=1` has `R=0` and `B=2`; no zero-trace condition was imposed. A cutoff of `v(s)=s^{-1/2}/log(e/s)` near zero is in `L²` but has `B=∞`, forcing `R=∞`. For `v_n(s)=e^{ins}φ(s)` with fixed smooth compactly supported `φ`, the `H^log` norm grows like `log n`, so the equality does not imply all `L²` functions have finite energy. Affine rescaling changes positive constants and bounded `L²` shifts, not the domain.
