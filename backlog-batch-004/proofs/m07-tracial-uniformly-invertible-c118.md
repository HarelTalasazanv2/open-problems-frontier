## Exact statement, normalization and foundations

Let(𝒬,τ) be any finite von Neumann algebra with faithful normal tracial **state**, τ(1)=1. Let0<p<1,q=1−p and A,B∈𝒬 be bounded positive operators satisfying A≥a_-1 and B≥b_-1 for positive a_-,b_-. Choose finite upper bounds A≤a_+1,B≤b_+1. Bounds may depend on this pair. Put X=A−B,M=pA+qB,φ(s)=s ln s, and

\[
 H_p=p\tau\varphi(A)+q\tau\varphi(B)-\tau\varphi(M),\qquad
 J_p=pq\tau[X(\ln A-\ln B)].
\]

Use exactly116's scalar positive-pair variational infimum

\[
 a_p=\inf_{r>0,r\ne1}\frac{J_p(r,1)}{2H_p(r,1)}\in[0,\infty).
\]

The claim is J_p≥2a_pH_p for every admitted algebra/pair. Scalar auxiliaries ℂ give equality of the infimum over this **bounded uniformly invertible tracial class** with a_p. For the nonnegative generator L=c(I−E_π), π=(p,q), c>0, its constant on this class is ca_p in the native2αD≤production convention.

Foundations used in full stated form: a unital commutative C*-algebra generated by commuting bounded self-adjoint operators has the continuous joint functional calculus, a contractive unital *-homomorphism from continuous functions on their compact joint spectrum; a bounded self-adjoint operator on any Hilbert space has a projection-valued spectral measure, whose vector measures are finite positive measures. ## Left/right multiplication on L²(𝒬,τ)

Complete 𝒬 in the norm ‖Y‖₂²=τ(Y*Y), with inner product ⟨Y,Z⟩=τ(Y*Z), obtaining 𝓗=L²(𝒬,τ). Faithfulness makes the initial embedding injective; e=1 is a vector of norm1. For bounded C∈𝒬, define L_CY=CY and R_CY=YC initially on the dense subspace𝒬.

They extend boundedly with operator norm≤‖C‖. For L_C this follows from C*C≤‖C‖²1. For R_C, trace cyclicity gives τ(C*Y*YC)=τ(Y*YCC*)≤‖C‖²τ(Y*Y), using positivity of the trace. If C=C*, the adjoint identities on the dense subspace, and trace cyclicity for R_C, show both extensions self-adjoint. If δ1≤C≤U1, then

\[
 \delta I\le L_C,R_C\le UI
\]

as Hilbert-space operators; for example ⟨Y,R_CY⟩=τ(YCY*)≥δτ(YY*)=δ‖Y‖₂². Left and right multiplication commute on the dense subspace and therefore on𝓗. This uses no finite basis or trace on B(𝓗). Linearity and the bounds persist on completion, whether or not𝓗 is separable.

## Norm-resolvent derivatives and the trace Hessian

Let C_t=B+tX, t∈[0,1], and F(t)=τ[C_t lnC_t]. With δ=min(a_-,b_-)>0, C_t≥δ1. A slightly larger t-interval retains a positive lower bound, since a perturbation hX has norm≤|h|‖X‖; X=0 is constant. The scalar logarithm representation lifts in the continuous functional calculus of C_t to the norm-convergent identity

\[
 \ln C_t=\int_0^\infty[(1+s)^{-1}1-(C_t+s1)^{-1}]ds.
\]

For a fixed lower bound δ and upper bound U, the tail is controlled by ‖C_t−1‖/[(1+s)(δ+s)], uniformly in t on a compact interval. Inverse differentiation follows directly from the resolvent identity, with derivative integrand bounded by ‖X‖/(δ+s)². On the slightly larger interval the same argument uses its smaller positive bound. Norm dominated convergence therefore yields

\[
 \frac{d}{dt}\ln C_t=\int_0^\infty R_{t,s}XR_{t,s}\,ds,
 \quad R_{t,s}=(C_t+s1)^{-1}.
\]

The bounded linear functional τ passes through these norm integrals. The product rule and cyclicity give

\[
 F'(t)=\tau[(\ln C_t+1)X],\qquad
 F''(t)=\int_0^\infty\tau[X R_{t,s}X R_{t,s}]ds. \tag{1}
\]

To check the first formula without merely invoking trace analyticity, move X to the end in τ[C_tR_{t,s}XR_{t,s}] and use ∫₀∞C_t(C_t+s1)^(−2)ds=1. That scalar identity lifts uniformly on σ(C_t)⊂[δ,U], including its tail. Thus the extra product-rule term is τX. The bounds also give continuous second derivatives, sufficient for the Green identity below. There is no unbounded logarithm in this admitted class.

## The joint-calculus lift replaces matrix units

Fix t and abbreviate L=L_(C_t),R=R_(C_t). They are commuting bounded self-adjoint operators with δI≤L,R≤UI. Their generated unital C*-algebra is commutative. Its compact joint spectrum Σ lies in[δ,U]², and the continuous joint calculus Θ sends the coordinate functions to L,R and is contractive.

On this rectangle the continuous scalar functions satisfy

\[
 \int_0^\infty\frac{ds}{(x+s)(y+s)}
 =\int_0^1\frac{d\theta}{(1-\theta)x+\theta y}. \tag{2}
\]

For x≠y both sides are(lnx−lny)/(x−y); for x=y both are1/x. The left tail beyond S is uniformly≤1/(δ+S). The right integrand is jointly continuous on its compact parameter domain with denominator≥δ. On finite intervals, bounded linear maps commute with norm Riemann/Bochner integrals; the uniform tail permits the improper limit. Applying Θ proves, in operator norm,

\[
 \int_0^\infty(L+sI)^{-1}(R+sI)^{-1}ds
 =\int_0^1[(1-\theta)L+\theta R]^{-1}d\theta. \tag{3}
\]

This is an equality of the continuous functions restricted to Σ and their operator images. It does not require Σ or either spectrum to be discrete, nor require the algebra𝒬 to be approximable by matrices.

Define on𝓗

\[
 P_\theta=(1-\theta)L_B+\theta R_B,\qquad
 Q_\theta=(1-\theta)L_A+\theta R_A.
\]

The inverse product on the left of(3), applied to X, is exactly R_(t,s)XR_(t,s). Using(1), the Hilbert inner product and linearity gives

\[
 F''(t)=\int_0^1\langle X,[(1-t)P_\theta+tQ_\theta]^{-1}X\rangle d\theta. \tag{4}
\]

P_θ and Q_θ need not commute. Their strict positive bounds b_-I≤P_θ≤b_+I and a_-I≤Q_θ≤a_+I are valid for every θ.

## Coherent pencil and possibly continuous spectral measure

The following algebra works for bounded uniformly positive P,Q on any Hilbert space. If X=(Q−P)e, set K=P^(−1/2)QP^(−1/2) and v=P^(1/2)e. Congruence and multiplication give

\[
 [(1-t)P+tQ]^{-1}=P^{-1/2}[(1-t)I+tK]^{-1}P^{-1/2},\quad
 P^{-1/2}X=(K-I)v.
\]

Let ν(S)=⟨v,E_K(S)v⟩ be the positive finite vector measure of the bounded self-adjoint K. Spectral calculus now proves

\[
 \langle X,[(1-t)P+tQ]^{-1}X\rangle
 =\int\frac{(k-1)^2}{1-t+tk}\,d\nu(k). \tag{5}
\]

For(4), take e=1. Then P_θe=B,Q_θe=A, so the required coherent relation X=(Q_θ−P_θ)e holds exactly. Put K_θ=P_θ^(−1/2)Q_θP_θ^(−1/2),v_θ=P_θ^(1/2)e and ν_θ its vector measure. For every θ,

\[
 \nu_\theta(\mathbb R)=\|v_\theta\|_2^2=\tau B,
 \quad \sigma(K_\theta)\subset[\ell,u]
 =[a_-/b_+,\ a_+/b_-]\subset(0,\infty). \tag{6}
\]

The bounds follow by applying congruence to a_-I≤Q_θ≤a_+I and the inverse bounds for P_θ. The first moment displacement is ∫(k−1)dν_θ=τ(A−B), although not needed for the inequality.

A separate polynomial check verifies the draft's stronger vector identification, without assuming a finite eigenbasis or simply repeating a reducing-subspace assertion. For every n≥0, P_θ^ne=B^n, since left and right B act identically on powers of B. Uniform polynomial approximation to square root on[b_-,b_+] therefore gives P_θ^(1/2)e=B^(1/2) in𝓗. Norm convergence in𝒬 implies L²convergence because τ is a state. The generic coherent-vector proof already works without this identification.

## Integration with continuous spectra, including signed deficits

P_θ,Q_θ,K_θ and v_θ are norm-continuous in θ. For square roots and inverse square roots this follows from uniform polynomial approximation on their fixed positive spectral intervals. For each continuous f on[ℓ,u], f(K_θ) is norm-continuous by the same approximation argument, hence

θ↦∫f(k)dν_θ(k)=⟨v_θ,f(K_θ)v_θ⟩

is continuous. This establishes the required weak continuity even through spectral degeneracies and continuous spectra, without selecting eigenvectors.

For t∈[0,1], k∈[ℓ,u],

\[
 1-t+tk\ge d:=\min(1,\ell)>0,\quad
 0\le h(t,k):=\frac{(k-1)^2}{1-t+tk}\le
 C_h:=\frac{\max(|\ell-1|,|u-1|)^2}{d}.
\]

The function h is jointly continuous on that rectangle. For any continuous bounded weight w(t), (t,θ)↦⟨v_θ,w(t)h(t,K_θ)v_θ⟩ is continuous: use uniform continuity in t and continuous functional calculus in θ. Ordinary scalar Fubini applies on the compact(t,θ) square. For each θ, the positive measure has finite mass τB, so its integral is a bounded linear functional on C([ℓ,u]); it commutes with the t-integral by uniform Riemann approximation. This justifies interchange with the spectral integral as well. Absolute values are bounded by ‖w‖_∞C_hτB, not merely by an argument valid for positive w. A joint finite sum of atoms is nowhere required.

## Green function and the scalar comparison

Let G_p(t)=qt for0≤t≤p and G_p(t)=p(1−t) for p≤t≤1. Integration by parts on the two intervals gives

\[
 H_p=\int_0^1G_p(t)F''(t)dt,
 \qquad J_p=pq\int_0^1F''(t)dt. \tag{7}
\]

Here C_p=M, and F′(1)−F′(0)=τ[X(lnA−lnB)]. The middle derivative boundary terms cancel because G_p(p)=pq. Thus the kernel is at p, not q, and its entropy coefficient is the native2α.

Combining(4)–(7), with the bounded signed weight w(t)=pq−2αG_p(t), proves for every real finite α

\[
 J_p(A,B)-2\alpha H_p(A,B)
 =\int_0^1\int[J_p(k,1)-2\alpha H_p(k,1)]\,d\nu_\theta(k)d\theta. \tag{8}
\]

To identify the inner integrand, apply the same elementary Green identities to g_k(t)=φ(1−t+tk), whose second derivative is h(t,k). This uses no assumed MLSI theorem. At k=1 both scalar terms vanish. Since a_p is finite and nonnegative, at α=a_p each scalar deficit is nonnegative by the defining infimum for k≠1, and at k=1 it is zero. Positive spectral measures prove the desired inequality.

If A≠B then X≠0. The integrand in(1) equals τ[(R_(t,s)^(1/2)XR_(t,s)^(1/2))²]>0 by faithfulness and invertibility. Hence F″(t)>0 and H_p>0. If A=B both H_p,J_p vanish. The ratio is used only at H_p>0. No small-denominator threshold or boundary limit is hidden.

The scalar algebraℂ and its positive pairs belong to the admitted class, so its quotients give the opposite bound on the class infimum. Common scaling reduces every scalar pair to(r,1); no attainment of the infimum is needed. Thus the infimum over all admitted algebras/pairs is exactly a_p.

## Generator and density conventions
